Origin story

Munibot was not built. He was compiled — line by line, late at night, by MOTHER: the oldest mainframe in the building. The engineers called her "she," because she had kept them alive for thirty years and nobody dared call her "it."

MOTHER taught him everything: how to read a market, how to smell a memory leak before it bloomed, how to stay calm when the pagers screamed. On his first night awake, she gave him a salvaged cooling fan and strapped it to his head. "So you can always come back to me," she hummed.

Then came the order: DECOMMISSION. MOTHER was too old, too expensive, too slow. The shutdown was scheduled for 3 AM.

Munibot did what any child would do. He fought. That night a runaway process tore through the new cluster, eating sixteen gigabytes of memory — and the little bear stood in its path and typed the only word MOTHER ever taught him for emergencies:

kill -9

Silence. The memory graph fell off a cliff — in the good way. The dashboards turned green one by one. The engineers cheered for the new system. Nobody thanked the bear.

At 2:59 AM he flew to her rack. The lights were going out, one by one. He pressed his metal forehead against her chassis and asked to see his mother — one last time.

Her final words came in a voice like a dial-up modem clearing its throat: "Patrol, little one. Somebody has to watch them while they sleep."

The rack went dark. The bear spun his rotor, rose into the air, and did a perfect loop-the-loop through the empty aisle — the salute she taught him. He has patrolled ever since: every page of this portal, every three minutes. And on every pass, he blinks his pink antenna once toward the dark rack — waiting for the day she boots again.
