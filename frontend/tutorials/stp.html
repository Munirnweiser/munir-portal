Straight-Through Processing

What STP really means, why trades break, and where support teams spend their mornings.

What STP means

Straight-through processing means a trade flows from order entry to settlement with zero manual intervention — no retyping, no spreadsheets, no phone calls. Every system in the chain reads the same electronic message and acts on it. A high STP rate (95%+ is the industry target) means lower cost, fewer errors, and faster settlement. A low one means an operations team doing by hand what machines should do.

STP is not one system; it is a property of the whole chain: execution → booking → confirmation → clearing → settlement → reconciliation. One weak link breaks the chain for that trade.

Why breaks happen

Almost every STP break is a data mismatch between two systems that each believe they are right. The usual suspects:

  • Economics mismatch — price, quantity or value date differs between counterparties' confirmations. A 1-cent price difference on a block trade is a break.
  • SSI mismatch — standing settlement instructions disagree: wrong custodian account, wrong agent bank, outdated details after a client migration.
  • Reference data gaps — unknown counterparty code, missing security static, unmapped account. New clients and new products break here first.
  • Booking errors — wrong trade date, wrong book, fat-fingered quantity; the front office booked it, the back office cannot recognize it.
  • Cutoff misses — instructions arriving after the custodian or CSD cutoff roll to the next cycle and age as fails.

Worked example. Desk A sells EUR 10M of a bond to Counterparty B at 98.50, value T+2. B's confirmation says 98.51 — a one-cent difference, about EUR 1,000 on the notional. Matching fails, both sides' operations teams investigate, and the trade sits unmatched until someone concedes or amends. Multiply by hundreds of trades on a busy day.

The break lifecycle

Breaks age in buckets — T+0, T+1, T+2, older — and each bucket has an owner and an escalation path. Affirmation platforms (and matching utilities) auto-match what they can; the residue lands in break queues. Support's job is triage: is this a one-off data issue, a systematic feed problem, or a counterparty outage? A single systematic cause can hide behind fifty individual breaks.

What production support watches

  • STP rate dashboards — a sudden dip is the earliest signal of a feed, reference-data or counterparty problem.
  • Aging breaks — breaks older than T+2 approaching settlement need active chasing; fails cost money and regulatory attention.
  • Matching engine health — queue depth, match latency, and exception rates on the confirmation/matching platforms.
  • Reference data loads — the overnight static-data batch is the foundation of the next day's STP; a failed load means a morning of breaks.
