Straight-Through Processing

What STP really means, why trades break, and where support teams spend their mornings.

What STP means

Straight-through processing means a trade flows from order entry to settlement with zero manual intervention — no retyping, no spreadsheets, no phone calls. Every system in the chain reads the same electronic message and acts on it. A high STP rate (95%+ is the industry target) means lower cost, fewer errors, and faster settlement. A low one means an operations team doing by hand what machines should do.

STP is not one system; it is a property of the whole chain: execution → booking → confirmation → clearing → settlement → reconciliation. One weak link breaks the chain for that trade.

Why breaks happen

Almost every STP break is a data mismatch between two systems that each believe they are right. The usual suspects:

  • Economics mismatch — price, quantity or value date differs between counterparties' confirmations. A 1-cent price difference on a block trade is a break.
  • SSI mismatch — standing settlement instructions disagree: wrong custodian account, wrong agent bank, outdated details after a client migration.
  • Reference data gaps — unknown counterparty code, missing security static, unmapped account. New clients and new products break here first.
  • Booking errors — wrong trade date, wrong book, fat-fingered quantity; the front office booked it, the back office cannot recognize it.
  • Cutoff misses — instructions arriving after the custodian or CSD cutoff roll to the next cycle and age as fails.

Worked example. Desk A sells EUR 10M of a bond to Counterparty B at 98.50, value T+2. B's confirmation says 98.51 — a one-cent difference, about EUR 1,000 on the notional. Matching fails, both sides' operations teams investigate, and the trade sits unmatched until someone concedes or amends. Multiply by hundreds of trades on a busy day.

The break lifecycle

Breaks age in buckets — T+0, T+1, T+2, older — and each bucket has an owner and an escalation path. Affirmation platforms (and matching utilities) auto-match what they can; the residue lands in break queues. Support's job is triage: is this a one-off data issue, a systematic feed problem, or a counterparty outage? A single systematic cause can hide behind fifty individual breaks.

What production support watches

  • STP rate dashboards — a sudden dip is the earliest signal of a feed, reference-data or counterparty problem.
  • Aging breaks — breaks older than T+2 approaching settlement need active chasing; fails cost money and regulatory attention.
  • Matching engine health — queue depth, match latency, and exception rates on the confirmation/matching platforms.
  • Reference data loads — the overnight static-data batch is the foundation of the next day's STP; a failed load means a morning of breaks.

SSI enrichment: the unglamorous king of settlement

Standing Settlement Instructions tell the world where to deliver your cash and securities: custodian, agent bank, account number, BIC, place of settlement (PSET). Every trade must be enriched with the right SSI before it can settle — and "right" depends on currency, instrument, counterparty, and sometimes the trading desk. The industry SSI utility is ALERT (part of DTCC), where the street publishes and consumes SSIs electronically instead of faxing them.

  • Effective dating: SSIs change — mergers, custodian migrations, new accounts. An instruction enriched with yesterday's SSI fails today. Every SSI record carries effective dates, and the enrichment engine must pick the one valid on value date, not trade date.
  • SSI waterfalls: when no exact SSI matches, engines fall back through a hierarchy (counterparty → agent → default). A wrong fallback delivers to the wrong account — technically "settled", practically a fail.
  • The classic ticket: "trades failing with counterparty X since Monday" — root cause is almost always an SSI change on their side that nobody told operations about. First check: compare the SSI used against ALERT's current record.

Affirmation platforms: CTM and the matching machine

For cross-border institutional trades, affirmation runs on CTM (DTCC's Central Trade Manager, successor to Omgeo Oasys/Alert). The workflow:

  • 1. Submit — both sides (or their outsourcers) submit trade details: economics, accounts, SSIs, deadlines.
  • 2. Match — CTM compares every field against tolerance rules. Price, quantity and dates must agree exactly; some narrative fields tolerate fuzz.
  • 3. Affirm — the investment manager affirms the broker's block and allocations; affirmed trades flow to settlement.
  • 4. Exceptions — mismatches land in the exception queue with reason codes (MISM = economics mismatch, MDIA = missing SSI, etc.).

Support reality. A new fund launches Monday; by Wednesday its trades sit in CTM exceptions with "unknown account" — the fund's accounts were never set up in the affirmation static. One reference-data ticket, fifty trades unblocked. Support watches: CTM connectivity and file/API ingestion, match-rate dashboards by counterparty, exception aging, and the affirmation deadline clock — un-affirmed by cutoff means manual chasing.

Break-resolution SLA workflow

Mature operations teams run breaks like an incident queue, with SLAs by age and type:

  • T+0 (same day): economics mismatches — resolve or amend same-day; owner: trade support. Target: 90% cleared by close.
  • T+1: SSI and reference-data breaks — fix static, re-enrich, resubmit. Target: cleared before settlement cutoffs.
  • T+2 and older: escalate to desk head and counterparty operations; daily aging report to management. Fails here accrue funding cost and regulatory attention.
  • Root-cause coding: every resolved break gets a code (data, counterparty, system, human). Weekly review of code frequencies turns firefighting into prevention — three weeks of "SSI" codes means an SSI project, not fifty one-off fixes.

Support watches: SLA breach counts (the morning ops meeting runs on them), re-break rates (fixed trades that break again signal a systematic cause), and the handoff between the overnight batch window and the morning shift — breaks born at 3 AM need owners by 8 AM.
